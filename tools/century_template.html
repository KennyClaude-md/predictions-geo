Why rates, not probabilities

The companion 2026–2036 forecast asks analysts for P(at least once by date T). That works over a decade and fails over a century — and this project has already measured the failure rather than assuming it.

The century in aggregate

Thresholds are on deaths, not on the 0–10 impact rating, because the rating saturates: a full strategic nuclear exchange and a Taiwan invasion both score 10, and they are not the same event. Deaths do not saturate.

The frequency–severity map

Every hazard placed by how often it recurs and how many it kills. Faceted by category rather than coloured by it — eight colours in one scatter cannot be told apart by a colourblind reader, and folding them into panels loses nothing. Filled marks rest on a counted record; hollow marks are judgement.

Expected occurrences per decade

Rates are not held constant across the century. Asteroid flux is flat; lethal heat frequency is not. Each hazard ramps its own rate, and this integrates them.

Counted rates and judged rates

The single most important distinction on this page. A megathrust interval rests on paleoseismology with a real event count. A nuclear-war interval rests on judgement with a number attached. Both appear below; only one is measurement.
